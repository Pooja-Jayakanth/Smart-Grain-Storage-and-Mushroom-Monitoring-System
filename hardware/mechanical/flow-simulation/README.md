# Thermal and Airflow Assessment of the Sensing Node Enclosure

**Smart Grain Storage and Mushroom Monitoring System**  
**Team Sisyphus**  
**Analysis software:** SOLIDWORKS Flow Simulation 2024 SP1  
**Study:** Natural convection and thermal interaction between the electronics enclosure and the ambient temperature and humidity sensor

## 1. Overview

This study evaluates the thermal performance of a wall mounted sensing node intended for monitoring the ambient conditions of a paddy storage facility. The mechanical design places the temperature and relative humidity sensor inside a ventilated holder beneath the main electronics enclosure. The primary objective is to determine whether heat generated by the electronics introduces a significant bias into the air temperature and relative humidity measured at the sensing location.

A steady state external flow simulation was conducted for a representative indoor environment of **32 °C and 70% relative humidity**. Heat transfer through the enclosure solids, buoyancy driven airflow and surface radiation were included. The electronics were assigned an estimated continuous heat dissipation of **1.35 W**.

Under these specific assumptions, the simulated sensor surface temperature was **32.12 °C**, compared with an ambient temperature of **32.00 °C**. Relative humidity in the sensor holder was **68.9% to 69.7%**, compared with an ambient value of **70%**. These predictions support the selected sensor placement for the simulated operating condition. They do not replace physical calibration or validation in a storage facility.

## 2. Engineering Objective

The electronics enclosure and the environmental sensor perform different thermal functions. The enclosure must protect and support the circuit board, while the sensor holder must remain exposed to representative room air. Locating the sensor too close to a warm electronic component, or in the natural convection plume leaving the enclosure, may cause erroneous readings.

The study therefore examines the following design requirements.

1. Minimise thermal disturbance at the temperature and humidity sensing location.
2. Establish the direction and magnitude of buoyancy driven airflow around the enclosure.
3. Assess the predicted temperatures of the enclosure and the electronic components under an assumed continuous heat load.
4. Identify limitations that must be addressed before deployment in a real grain storage environment.

| Feature | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Main enclosure | Ventilated, 3D printed housing for the PCB, regulator, battery and associated components |
| Sensor housing | Louvred holder positioned below the main electronics enclosure |
| Mounting orientation | Suspended from the upper mounting tab, with the sensor holder directed downward |

| Modelled electronics | Earlier ESP 12F based PCB configuration |

The downward sensor position is an intentional mechanical feature. Natural convection carries heated air upward from the electronics, reducing direct exposure of the sensor holder to the warm plume in the analysed hanging orientation.

## 4. Numerical Model and Boundary Conditions

The numerical study used a steady state external fluid domain containing humid air. Gravity, conduction through solids and thermal radiation were enabled. A zero ambient velocity was prescribed to examine natural convection in still indoor air.

| Parameter | Applied value or assumption |
| :--- | :--- |
| Analysis type | External, steady state flow with heat transfer |
| Fluid | Humid air; laminar and turbulent flow enabled |
| Ambient temperature | 32 °C |
| Ambient relative humidity | 70% |
| Ambient pressure | 101,325 Pa |
| Initial air velocity | 0 m/s |
| Gravity | 9.81 m/s² in the positive model Y direction, toward the sensor holder |
| Radiation | Enabled; surrounding radiation temperature 32 °C |
| Surface emissivity | 0.90 for the printed surfaces |
| Solid heat conduction | Enabled |
| PLA+ density | 1,230 kg/m³ |
| PLA+ thermal conductivity | 0.15 W/(m·K) |
| PLA+ specific heat capacity | 1,800 J/(kg·K) |
| PLA+ softening reference | 55 °C, used as a conservative warning threshold in the model |
| PCB substrate | FR4 |
| Initial mesh | Level 5 with a specified minimum gap of 1.5 mm |
| Total mesh cells | 3,158,364 |
| Solver completion | 382 iterations with the selected numerical goals converged |

The material properties are representative engineering assumptions, rather than measured properties of the manufactured print. The 55 °C reference is a **softening warning threshold**, not a measured melting temperature for the actual filament.

## 5. Simulation Results

### 5.1 Temperature and Humidity Near the Sensor

| Quantity | Simulation result | Ambient reference | Difference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sensor surface temperature | 32.12 °C | 32.00 °C | +0.12 °C |
| Air temperature inside sensor holder | 32.08 °C to 32.27 °C | 32.00 °C | +0.08 °C to +0.27 °C |
| Relative humidity inside sensor holder | 68.9% to 69.7% | 70.0% | −1.1 to −0.3 percentage points |
| Air near PCB inside enclosure | 41.5 °C | 32.00 °C | +9.5 °C |
| Rising air plume above enclosure | 35.0 °C to 35.7 °C | 32.00 °C | +3.0 °C to +3.7 °C |

The temperatures within the sensor holder remain close to ambient in the simulated arrangement, despite the higher temperatures present within the electronics enclosure. This suggests that the lower holder is largely outside the warm rising airflow.

### 5.2 Airflow and Thermal Behaviour

The simulated rising plume reaches approximately **0.08 m/s to 0.19 m/s** at the reported measurement positions above the enclosure. In contrast, airflow within the sensor holder is comparatively weak, with recorded local speeds of approximately **0.001 m/s to 0.014 m/s**.

The predicted maximum temperature of the printed enclosure material is approximately **50.4 °C**, leaving a difference of approximately **4.6 °C** below the assumed 55 °C softening reference at 32 °C ambient. This limited margin warrants further investigation at higher ambient temperatures. The reported ESP 12F maximum of **82.5 °C** is a numerical result from the simplified electronic model and should not be interpreted as independent verification of component thermal safety.

The recorded energy balance indicates approximately **0.70 W** associated with convection and **0.65 W** associated with radiation. These are interpreted values from the simulation goals and should be treated as approximate.

## 6. Effect of Sensor Placement

An earlier configuration placed the sensor above the electronics enclosure. That analysis indicated a sensor temperature of approximately **49 °C** and relative humidity of approximately **26% to 31%**, demonstrating the potential for substantial thermal interference when the sensor lies in the heated airflow path.

The earlier analysis used different material and radiation settings. Consequently, the numerical results from the two configurations **must not be treated as a controlled comparison**. Nevertheless, the studies provide a useful qualitative explanation for adopting the final arrangement with the sensor below the enclosure.

## 7. Engineering Assessment and Limitations

The results support the feasibility of the selected sensor position for the prescribed still air condition. The following qualifications are necessary when interpreting the study.

1. The analysis represents a single steady state indoor condition at 32 °C. Higher ambient temperatures and transient heating have not been evaluated.
2. The external domain does not include a storage wall, grain bags, shelving or obstructions that may modify the surrounding airflow.
3. The humidity sensor measures local ambient air in the holder. The simulation does not predict moisture or temperature inside stored grain.
4. Electronic heat generation was estimated using an earlier ESP 12F PCB configuration. The final ESP32 board requires an updated thermal assessment.
5. Some highly localised PCB temperatures are sensitive to simplified geometry and material assignments. They cannot be used to establish detailed component operating margins.
6. Numerical goal convergence was achieved, but a formal mesh independence study has not been documented.
7. No physical measurements against a calibrated temperature and humidity reference are included in this study.
8. The model does not represent the higher humidity and different environmental conditions expected in a mushroom growing area.

For deployment, the proposed enclosure should also be evaluated for dust ingress, condensation, mechanical mounting strength, maintenance access and material durability. The present Flow Simulation study addresses thermal and airflow behaviour only.

## 9. Reproduction Procedure

1. Obtain the complete [`Model/`](Model/) directory, including the assembly and all referenced parts and subassemblies.
2. Open `Node enclosure_flow.SLDASM` in SOLIDWORKS 2024 with the Flow Simulation add in enabled.
3. Check that all referenced parts resolve correctly and that the enclosure has the documented hanging orientation.
4. If the saved Flow Simulation study is available, review its settings. Otherwise recreate the numerical setup using [`setup/simulation-setup.md`](setup/simulation-setup.md).
5. Confirm the user defined PLA+ material properties, radiation settings, heat sources, gravity direction, computational domain, mesh and numerical goals.
6. Run the flow and heat transfer analysis to convergence.
7. Import the applicable coordinates from [`setup/probe_points.csv`](setup/probe_points.csv) using the SOLIDWORKS Point Parameters tool. Omit or reposition the point located outside the computational domain.
8. Compare the resulting temperatures, relative humidity, air velocities and solid temperatures with the documented data in [`results/`](results/).

Reproduced results may differ slightly because of solver settings, mesh generation, material database configurations and software environment. The reported run required approximately **4 to 5 hours** on the machine used for the original study; this is not a guaranteed runtime.

## 10. Recommended Further Validation

A stronger validation programme should include a mesh sensitivity study, numerical cases at 38 °C and 40 °C, assessment of wall proximity, and tests with the measured power dissipation of the final ESP32 board. The completed physical enclosure should then be tested against a calibrated reference sensor under representative storage conditions. Mechanical testing of the printed mounting tab and inspection of the enclosure under high humidity should be addressed separately.

## 11. Conclusion

The simulated natural convection pattern supports positioning the temperature and humidity sensor **below the heat generating electronics enclosure**. For the prescribed ambient conditions and assumed power input, the local sensor temperature and relative humidity remain close to ambient values. The result is a useful engineering justification for the selected enclosure geometry, while the outstanding thermal, environmental and experimental checks must be completed before claiming full field validation.
